\section[Atmospheric Propagation and ACM]{Atmospheric Propagation and ACM\textsuperscript{*}\footnote{Chapters and sections marked with \textsuperscript{*} represent advanced, optional extensions developed beyond the core UZB451E term project specifications to model real-world satellite communication dynamics.}}

This chapter presents the standards-based atmospheric attenuation models and Adaptive Coding and Modulation (ACM) mechanisms implemented in this project. \textbf{These models are optional professional extensions representing an advanced study of link availability.}

\subsection[ITU-R Slant-Path Attenuation Suite]{ITU-R Slant-Path Attenuation Suite\textsuperscript{*}}
The standards-based availability study implements the slant-path propagation recommendations of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU-R). The total atmospheric attenuation $A_{total}(p)$ in dB exceeded for a time percentage $p$ ($0.001\% \le p \le 5\%$) is combined as recommended in \textit{ITU-R P.618-13 Section 2.5}~\cite{itu618}:
\begin{equation}
    A_{total}(p) = A_{gaseous} + \sqrt{ \left( A_{rain}(p) + A_{cloud} \right)^2 + A_{scintillation}(p)^2 }
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Yağış Sönümlemesi (ITU-R P.618, P.838, P.839)}
The rain attenuation $A_{rain}(p)$ is calculated by determining the rain height $h_R$ and slant-path length $L_s$ under the rain layer. The specific attenuation $\gamma_R$ in dB/km is computed using the horizontal and vertical power-law coefficients $k$ and $\alpha$ from \textit{ITU-R P.838-3}~\cite{itu838}:
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_R = k R_{0.01}^\alpha
\end{equation}
where $R_{0.01}$ is the local point rainfall rate exceeded for $0.01\%$ of an average year in mm/h. The horizontal projection $L_G$ and reduction factor $r_{0.01}$ are computed to account for the finite spatial cell-size of rain cells. The vertical adjustment factor $v_{0.01}$ accounts for the slant path profile, yielding the effective path length $L_{eff} = L_R v_{0.01}$. The sönümleme $A_{0.01}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    A_{0.01} = \gamma_R L_{eff}
\end{equation}
For other percentages $p$, $A_p$ is scaled exponentially using a latitude- and elevation-dependent scaling factor:
\begin{equation}
    A_p = A_{0.01} \left( \frac{p}{0.01} \right)^{-\left[0.655 + 0.033\ln p - 0.045\ln A_{0.01} - \beta(1-p)\sin\theta\right]}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection[Atmosferik Gaz Emilimi (ITU-R P.676-12 Annex 2)]{Atmosferik Gaz Emilimi (ITU-R P.676-12 Annex 2)\textsuperscript{*}}
The gaseous attenuation $A_{gaseous}$ accounts for dry air (oxygen) and water vapour absorption. Using surface pressure, temperature, and absolute humidity, specific zenith attenuations $\gamma_o$ and $\gamma_w$ are computed alongside their equivalent height limits $h_o$ and $h_w$ according to \textit{ITU-R P.676-12}~\cite{itu676}:
\begin{equation}
    A_{gaseous} = \frac{\gamma_o h_o + \gamma_w h_w}{\sin\theta}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection[Bulut Sıvı Su Sönümlemesi (ITU-R P.840-8)]{Bulut Sıvı Su Sönümlemesi (ITU-R P.840-8)\textsuperscript{*}}
Attenuation due to cloud liquid water $A_{cloud}$ uses Rayleigh scattering with a double-Debye model for water dielectric permittivity, yielding a specific attenuation coefficient $K_l$ in $(\text{dB/km})/(\text{g/m}^3)$ scaled by the columnar liquid water $M$ in kg/m$^2$ according to \textit{ITU-R P.840-8}~\cite{itu840}:
\begin{equation}
    A_{cloud} = \frac{M \cdot K_l}{\sin\theta}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection[Troposferik Sintilasyon Sönümlemesi (ITU-R P.618-13 Sec. 2.4.1)]{Troposferik Sintilasyon Sönümlemesi (ITU-R P.618-13 Sec. 2.4.1)\textsuperscript{*}}
Tropospheric scintillation $A_{scintillation}(p)$ represents fast signal standard-deviation variations $\sigma$ caused by local atmospheric turbulence, scaled by the aperture-averaging factor $g(x)$ of the receive dish antenna:
\begin{equation}
    A_{scintillation}(p) = a(p) \sigma
\end{equation}
where $a(p)$ is a time-percentage factor.

\subsection[Monte-Carlo Yağış Outage Simülasyonu]{Monte-Carlo Yağış Outage Simülasyonu\textsuperscript{*}}
While the ITU-R recommendations provide deterministic, standards-based bounds for system availability, understanding the true statistical distribution of link outages requires stochastic modeling. To evaluate the link margin under dynamic weather events, an advanced Monte Carlo simulation engine was developed. 

The simulation executes $N = 8760$ discrete trials, representing hourly atmospheric states over an entire meteorological year. At each time step $t_i$, the atmospheric state $S(t_i)$ is sampled from an empirical probability mass function representing local climatology:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clear-Sky ($S_0$):} Represents standard operating conditions ($0$~dB excess attenuation). Occurs with high probability (e.g., $90\%$).
    \item \textbf{Light and Moderate Rain ($S_1, S_2$):} Introduces small to medium fade depths, drawn from a uniformly distributed attenuation profile.
    \item \textbf{Heavy Storms ($S_3$):} Rare events ($p < 0.5\%$) triggering severe attenuation (up to $15$~dB in Ku-band), modeled as a heavy-tailed stochastic variable.
\end{itemize}

For each generated weather state, the exact signal-to-noise ratio and link margin are instantaneously recalculated:
\begin{equation}
    Margin(t_i) = E_b/N_0(t_i) - \text{Required } E_b/N_0
\end{equation}
By aggregating the results across all $N$ trials, the system generates a massive statistical dataset. This dataset is then sorted to produce a Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of the link margin. An \textit{outage event} is mathematically defined as any trial where $Margin(t_i) < 0$. The overall system availability is directly computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Availability (\%)} = 100 \times \left( 1 - \frac{\sum \mathbf{1}(Margin(t_i) < 0)}{N} \right)
\end{equation}
This Monte Carlo framework provides a highly intuitive, distribution-based contrast to the rigid analytical curves of the ITU-R models, enabling robust risk assessment for satellite operators.

\subsection[DVB-S2 ACM (Adaptive Coding \& Modulation)]{DVB-S2 ACM (Adaptive Coding \& Modulation)\textsuperscript{*}}
In faded downlink channels, a fixed transmission format (MODCOD) either causes link outage or wastes capacity. The DVB-S2 ACM module models a receiver that monitors the measured $Es/N0$ and dynamically selects the highest spectral efficiency $\eta$ from the DVB-S2 MODCOD ladder that closes the link:
\begin{equation}
    Es/N0_{available} \ge Required\ Es/N0_{MODCOD} + Margin_{impl}
\end{equation}
The usable symbol rate is $R_s = B / (1 + \alpha_{rolloff})$, yielding a dynamic net throughput:
\begin{equation}
    Throughput(Mbps) = \eta \cdot R_s
\end{equation}
which optimizes the channel capacity under time-varying atmospheric fades according to the DVB-S2 standard~\cite{dvbs2}.
