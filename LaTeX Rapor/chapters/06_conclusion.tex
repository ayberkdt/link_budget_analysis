\section{Conclusion}

The report evaluated a 14/12~GHz transparent GEO link from Rome to Ankara through HOTBIRD 13G. The same Python model was used for the clear-sky budget, the optional impairment cases, and the ablation comparison.

In clear sky, the combined $C/N_0$ is \cnTotal~dB-Hz and the 10~Mbps fixed-rate margin is \marginClear~dB. The selected ASI and IMD terms reduce the margin to \marginASI~dB. At the 99.9\% atmospheric design point, the margin is \marginRain~dB; the stated baseline therefore does not close against the 7~dB fixed-rate threshold at that availability.

For the 36~MHz carrier and 0.20 roll-off factor, the ACM selector changes from QPSK 3/4 in clear sky to QPSK 1/2 at the atmospheric design point. The latter corresponds to an estimated \throughputRain~Mbps and remains a variable-rate mode, not evidence of 10~Mbps fixed-rate availability.

The largest modeled penalties come from rain attenuation and the associated increase in receive-system noise temperature. Candidate design changes are a larger GS2 aperture, higher contracted downlink EIRP, verified uplink power-control headroom, or a lower fixed-rate requirement. An operational design would also replace the shared representative rainfall rate with separate mapped or measured values for Rome and Ankara and would use transponder-specific EIRP, G/T, polarization, and loading data.

Intelsat 39 and other GEO spacecraft may be suitable for different coverage or connectivity objectives. For the sites and broadcast-oriented service considered here, HOTBIRD 13G offers the more balanced geometry and the more directly usable public footprint data.
