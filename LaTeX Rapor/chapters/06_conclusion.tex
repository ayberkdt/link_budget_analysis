\section{Conclusion}

This term project presented a rigorous, standards-compliant system engineering analysis of a geostationary bent-pipe satellite communication link operating at Ku-band. Connecting Ground Station 1 (Rome) to Ground Station 2 (Ankara) via the Eutelsat Hotbird 13G satellite, the study established a solid theoretical baseline and overlaid advanced physical layers, atmospheric physics, and modern adaptive mitigation techniques.

\subsection{Summary of Numerical Findings}
Through the object-oriented Python simulation suite, several critical performance metrics were computed. The calculations are based on standard link-budget equations and ITU-R propagation models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clear-Sky Baseline Performance:} The nominal clear-sky link budget closes with an end-to-end carrier-to-noise density ratio $(C/N_0)_{\text{total}}$ of $\cnTotal~\text{dB}\cdot\text{Hz}$, which corresponds to a received energy-per-bit to noise-power spectral density ratio $E_b/N_0$ of $\ebnoTotal~\text{dB}$. Under a required digital threshold of $\reqEbNo~\text{dB}$, the link possesses an open-loop clear-sky margin of $\marginClear~\text{dB}$.

    \item \textbf{Adjacent Satellite Interference (ASI):} Accounting for co-channel adjacent satellite interference and non-linear IMD, the link suffers an equivalent noise degradation. This reduces the operating clear-sky margin to $\marginASI~\text{dB}$.
    \item \textbf{Atmospheric Propagation Penalty:} The ITU-R P.618-13 propagation models predict that gaseous absorption, cloud attenuation, and rain-induced scattering introduce severe, dynamic propagation losses. At an annual availability target of $99.9\%$, the uplink total attenuation reaches $\upRainAtten~\text{dB}$ at $14.0~\text{GHz}$, while the downlink total attenuation is $\downRainAtten~\text{dB}$ at $12.0~\text{GHz}$. These atmospheric fades plunge the operational link margin to $\marginRain~\text{dB}$.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Coding and Modulation (ACM):} The ACM selector attempts to preserve service by downshifting to QPSK 2/5; however, because the 99.9\% rain-fade margin remains negative, strict link closure still requires additional RF margin, such as a larger GS2 dish, higher satellite EIRP, or uplink power control.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{System Engineering Recommendations}
Based on the parametric contour analysis and Monte Carlo simulations, the following design recommendations are proposed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Downlink Antenna Upgrade:} Ground terminals in high rain-rate zones ($R_{0.01} > 30~\text{mm/h}$) should utilise a minimum dish diameter of $1.2~\text{m}$. The current $\gsTwoDish$~m consumer dish provides minimal fade protection.
    \item \textbf{Uplink Power Control (ULPC):} The Rome uplink terminal should implement ULPC, dynamically raising HPA output from 20~W up to 100~W during localised storm events to pre-compensate the uplink rain attenuation.
    \item \textbf{Probabilistic Margin Planning:} Operators should transition from deterministic margins to probabilistic risk assessment. The Monte Carlo models developed here illustrate that a static $4~\text{dB}$ margin is insufficient for strict $99.9\%$ availability in Southern Europe and Anatolia.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Work}
Potential extensions of this study include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Regenerative On-Board Processing (OBP) to decouple uplink and downlink noise, yielding an estimated 2.5--3.0~dB improvement.
    \item High-Throughput spot-beam architectures with frequency reuse for higher aggregate capacity.
    \item Transition to Ka-band or Q/V-band, which offer larger bandwidth but require advanced site diversity and fast-acting ACM loops.
\end{itemize}
