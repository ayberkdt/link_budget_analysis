\section{Conclusion}

This term project presented a transparent, comprehensive, and highly robust analysis of a geostationary bent-pipe satellite communication link at Ku-band. 

\subsection{Project Findings}
Through the calculations implemented in our Python analyzer, the following key engineering outcomes were achieved:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline Validation:} The open-air static link budget closes with a combined $E_b/N_0$ of $10.93$ dB, yielding a link margin of $3.93$ dB under clear-sky conditions. This baseline is fully comparable to the MATLAB "Static Link Budget Analyzer" results (Include P.618 Losses left unticked), validating the structural correctness of the geometry and RF equations.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity Trade-offs:} The five 2D contour plots successfully demonstrated the core satellite design trade-offs: the positive impact of higher frequencies and antenna sizes on receive $C/N_0$, the power-bandwidth boundary for bit rate scaling, the adjacent satellite discrimination angle trends, and the geographical limits of elevation angles.
    \item \textbf{Adjacent-Satellite Interference (ASI):} The co-channel adjacent interference analysis showed a $0.50$ dB penalty on our final margin (reducing it to $3.43$ dB), proving that satellite spacing and antenna off-axis discrimination are critical design constraints.
    \item \textbf{Propagation and Availability:} The standards-based ITU-R propagation suite (P.618/P.676/P.840) demonstrated that atmospheric and rain attenuation impose significant slow fades ($3.83$ dB on the uplink and $2.65$ dB on the downlink at $99.9\%$ availability). This reduces our faded margin to $0.32$ dB, barely closing the link.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Mitigation:} The DVB-S2 Adaptive Coding and Modulation (ACM) successfully illustrated how the link can be protected. By dynamically shifting down to QPSK 1/2 during rain events, the transponder maintains connectivity with a net throughput of $29.66$ Mbps, instead of suffering a complete outage.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Final Remarks}
In conclusion, this project successfully bridges textbook theory from Pratt's \textit{Satellite Communications} with modern standards-compliant satellite system engineering. The modular Python structure ensures that the analyzer is easily adaptable for alternative coordinate configurations, frequency bands (e.g. Ka-band), or transponder parameters.
The clear distinction between the compulsory clear-sky baseline and the advanced professional extensions (ASI, dynamic noise, orbit motion, Monte Carlo simulations, ITU-R models, and ACM) showcases a comprehensive study of geostationary communication links.
