The end-to-end combined carrier-to-noise density ratio is computed using inverse-linear addition:
\begin{equation}
    \left(\frac{C}{N_0}\right)_{\mathrm{total,linear}}
    =
    \left(10^{-84.08/10}+10^{-84.25/10}\right)^{-1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \left(\frac{C}{N_0}\right)_{\mathrm{total,dBHz}}
    =
    10\log_{10}\left[
    \left(\frac{C}{N_0}\right)_{\mathrm{total,linear}}
    \right]
    =
    \cnTotal\,\mathrm{dBHz}
\end{equation}
This gives $E_b/N_0=\ebnoTotal$~dB and a 10~Mbps fixed-rate margin of \textbf{\marginClear~dB} against the 7~dB requirement.

\subsubsection{Parametric Sensitivity and 2D Contour Map Analysis}
Five contour plots show the principal static sensitivities and link-closure boundaries.

\subsubsection[Downlink carrier-to-noise density vs. GS2 dish diameter and frequency]{Downlink \texorpdfstring{$C/N_0$}{C/N0} vs. GS2 Dish Diameter and Frequency}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{01_downlink_cn0_dish_vs_frequency.png}
    \caption[Downlink $C/N_0$ vs. GS2 dish diameter and frequency.]{Downlink $C/N_0$ vs. GS2 dish diameter and frequency.}
    \label{fig:contour01}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Purpose:} Evaluate downlink $C/N_0$ over receive-dish diameters of 0.5--2.4~m and frequencies of 10.7--12.75~GHz.
    \item \textbf{Result:} $C/N_0$ ranges from approximately 81.5 to 91.5~dBHz over the plotted domain. The nearly vertical contours show that aperture is the dominant variable.
    \item \textbf{Interpretation:} For fixed physical aperture and EIRP, receive gain and FSPL both contain an $f^2$ dependence, so their frequency terms largely cancel. Frequency becomes important when atmospheric loss, efficiency, or footprint EIRP also varies with band.
    \item \textbf{Design use:} The plot gives the minimum receive aperture needed for a specified $C/N_0$ under the fixed assumptions.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection[Downlink Margin vs. Satellite EIRP and GS2 System Noise]{Downlink Margin vs. Satellite EIRP and GS2 $T_{\mathrm{sys}}$}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{02_downlink_margin_eirp_vs_tsys.png}
    \caption[Combined effective margin vs. satellite EIRP and GS2 $T_{\mathrm{sys}}$.]{Combined effective margin vs. satellite EIRP and GS2 $T_{\mathrm{sys}}$.}
    \label{fig:contour02}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Purpose:} Map the 0~dB end-to-end margin boundary over 42--58~dBW downlink EIRP and 90--330~K receiver noise temperature, with ASI and IMD enabled.
    \item \textbf{Numerical result:} The 0~dB contour separates closed and open combinations. At $T_{\mathrm{sys}}=300$~K, closure requires approximately 45.5~dBW. The selected 46~dBW, 150~K point gives approximately \marginASI~dB with ASI and IMD enabled.
    \item \textbf{Interpretation:} Margin increases one-for-one with EIRP in dB, while the receiver term varies as $-10\log_{10}T_{\mathrm{sys}}$.
    \item \textbf{Design use:} The map quantifies the trade between leased downlink EIRP and receive-terminal $(G/T)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection[Uplink carrier-to-noise ratio vs. HPA power and GS1 dish diameter]{Uplink \texorpdfstring{$C/N$}{C/N} vs. HPA Power and GS1 Dish Diameter}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{03_uplink_cn_power_vs_dish.png}
    \caption[Uplink $C/N$ vs. HPA power and GS1 dish diameter.]{Uplink $C/N$ vs. HPA power and GS1 dish diameter.}
    \label{fig:contour03}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Purpose:} Compare 2--80~W HPA output with 0.8--4.5~m uplink aperture.
    \item \textbf{Result:} The 2.4~m, 20~W baseline gives $C/N\approx8.51$~dB. A 12~dB target at the same aperture requires about 45~W. At 1.2~m, the additional 6~dB aperture penalty would require about 180~W, outside the plotted range.
    \item \textbf{Interpretation:} Doubling dish diameter adds approximately 6~dB of gain. For this case, aperture growth is more effective than increasing HPA output alone.
    \item \textbf{Design use:} The plot supports a cost and packaging trade between antenna size, amplifier rating, and off-axis discrimination.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection[Combined bit-energy ratio vs. information bit rate and bandwidth]{Combined \texorpdfstring{$E_b/N_0$}{Eb/N0} vs. Information Bit Rate and Bandwidth}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{04_total_ebn0_bitrate_vs_bandwidth.png}
    \caption[Effective end-to-end $E_b/(N_0+I_0)$ vs. information bit rate and bandwidth.]{Effective end-to-end $E_b/(N_0+I_0)$ vs. information bit rate and bandwidth.}
    \label{fig:contour04}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Purpose:} Evaluate effective end-to-end $E_b/N_0$ over 2--60~Mbps and 6--72~MHz.
    \item \textbf{Result:} With $C/N_0\approx81.15$~dBHz, the noise-only $E_b/N_0$ falls from about 18.1~dB at 2~Mbps to 4.2~dB at 50~Mbps. At 10~Mbps it is 11.15~dB, or 10.69~dB with ASI and IMD.
    \item \textbf{Interpretation:} At fixed $C/N_0$, $E_b/N_0$ depends on bit rate, not directly on allocated bandwidth. Bandwidth instead limits symbol rate, occupied spectrum, and feasible MODCODs.
    \item \textbf{Design use:} The plot identifies the fixed-rate closure boundary for the stated 7~dB requirement.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Advanced Impairment and Dynamic Analysis}
The advanced calculations retain the baseline hardware and geometry, changing only the active physical models.

\subsubsection[Interference (ASI and IMD)]{Interference (ASI and IMD)}
The modeled neighbors at $11^\circ$E and $15^\circ$E reduce the fixed-rate margin from \marginClear~dB to \marginASI~dB. Most of this change comes from ASI; the assumed 30~dB transponder IMD term contributes only 0.01~dB. These values are sensitivity assumptions, not coordination limits.

The controlled sequence identifies three materially different effects: ASI costs about 0.44~dB, rain carrier attenuation costs 2.41~dB at the coincident stress point, and downlink atmospheric emission costs a further 1.17~dB. The non-rain replacement and dynamic clear-sky receiver model slightly improve the result relative to the original fixed allowances. This decomposition removes the earlier ambiguity in which a 150~K-to-dynamic-temperature change was incorrectly attributed entirely to rain emission.
