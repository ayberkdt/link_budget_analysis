\begin{equation}
    \left( \frac{C}{N_0} \right)_{\text{total,linear}} = \left( 10^{-84.08 / 10} + 10^{-84.25 / 10} \right)^{-1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \left( \frac{C}{N_0} \right)_{\text{total,dBHz}} = 10\log_{10}\left( \left( \frac{C}{N_0} \right)_{\text{total,linear}} \right) = \cnTotal\ \text{dB-Hz}

The combined end-to-end fixed-rate margin is evaluated using a separate required end-to-end $E_b/N_0$ threshold of $7$ dB. This margin closes the link above the required digital threshold of $7.0$ dB under clear-sky conditions.

\subsubsection{Parametric Sensitivity and 2D Contour Map Analysis}
The custom Python analyzer generates five distinct contour maps to study static system sensitivities, parameter trade-offs, and design boundaries. Each figure is analyzed below using a structured evaluation.

\subsubsection{Downlink $(C/N_0)$ vs. GS2 Dish Diameter and Frequency}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{01_downlink_cn0_dish_vs_frequency.png}
    \caption{Downlink $(C/N_0)$ vs. GS2 dish diameter and frequency.}
    \label{fig:contour01}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Setup / Objective:} Investigate how the receiving antenna aperture size ($0.5$~m to $2.4$~m) and the downlink carrier frequency ($10.7$~GHz to $12.75$~GHz) jointly dictate the signal power density ($C/N_0$) at the receiver demodulator.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Observations:} The $C/N_0$ value scales from a minimum of approximately $81.5$~dB-Hz (small dish, lower end of the band) to a maximum of approximately $91.5$~dB-Hz (large dish, upper end of the band). The contour lines are nearly vertical, indicating that dish diameter is the dominant variable.
    \item \textbf{Physical / Engineering Interpretation:} For a fixed physical aperture and fixed satellite EIRP, receive antenna gain scales as $G_{\text{rx}} \propto (D f)^2$, while free-space path loss scales as $\text{FSPL} \propto f^2$. These two frequency-squared dependencies largely cancel, so the net $C/N_0$ is nearly independent of frequency when all other parameters are held constant. The nearly vertical contours in the figure confirm this expected cancellation. Frequency becomes a meaningful design driver only when atmospheric attenuation, antenna efficiency variation, satellite footprint EIRP variation, or band-dependent hardware assumptions are included.
    \item \textbf{Design Guidelines:} Ground station terminal designers utilize this map to size the minimum dish diameter required for specific satellite transponder bands, ensuring that the receiver $G/T$ remains high enough to close the link without paying for oversized hardware.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Downlink Margin vs. Satellite EIRP and GS2 $T_{\mathrm{sys}}$}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{02_downlink_margin_eirp_vs_tsys.png}
    \caption{Combined effective margin vs. satellite EIRP and GS2 $T_{\mathrm{sys}}$.}
    \label{fig:contour02}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Setup / Objective:} Map the combined end-to-end link margin boundary ($0$~dB limit) by trading off satellite transmit power (downlink EIRP, $42$~dBW to $58$~dBW) against the receiver noise performance ($T_{\mathrm{sys}}$, $90$~K to $330$~K). The plotted metric is the combined $E_b/(N_0+I_0)$ margin, which includes both thermal noise and the ASI/IMD interference budget active in the advanced analysis.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Observations:} A bold diverging-color boundary splits the parameter space at the $0$~dB margin line. Any combination in the positive-margin region represents successful link closure. For instance, at a high receiver noise temperature ($T_{\mathrm{sys}} = 300$~K), the link requires a minimum satellite EIRP of approximately $45.5$~dBW to close. The operating point marker at EIRP $= 46$~dBW and $T_{\mathrm{sys}} = 150$~K confirms the effective margin of approximately $\marginASI$~dB, consistent with the advanced-impaired result summarized in Table~\ref{tab:comparison_summary}.
    \item \textbf{Physical / Engineering Interpretation:} The combined margin varies linearly with satellite power in dB, but scales inversely with the logarithm of system noise temperature ($G/T \propto -10\log_{10} T_{\mathrm{sys}}$). Higher thermal noise in the receiver LNB directly degrades the signal-to-noise ratio, requiring a compensating increase in transponder power.
    \item \textbf{Design Guidelines:} This trade-off is critical for cost optimization. Satellite operators can decide whether to spend capital on highly powerful satellites (high EIRP) or to invest in high-performance ground terminals (low $T_{\mathrm{sys}}$ LNBs) to maintain a target link margin.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Uplink C/N vs. HPA Power and GS1 Dish Diameter}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{03_uplink_cn_power_vs_dish.png}
    \caption{Uplink C/N vs HPA Power and GS1 Dish Diameter.}
    \label{fig:contour03}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Setup / Objective:} Evaluate the trade-off between the uplink ground station High-Power Amplifier (HPA) wattage ($2$~W to $80$~W) and the uplink transmit antenna diameter ($0.8$~m to $4.5$~m) to achieve a target transponder Carrier-to-Noise ratio.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Observations:} At the baseline operating point of $2.4$~m dish and $20$~W HPA, the uplink $C/N$ is approximately $8.51$~dB. Reaching a $12$~dB uplink $C/N$ target with the same $2.4$~m dish would require approximately $45$~W of HPA power ($3.49$~dB additional gain). Reducing the dish to $1.2$~m introduces a further $6$~dB transmit-gain penalty, requiring approximately $180$~W, which lies outside the plotted range. Therefore, the contour map demonstrates that high uplink $C/N$ targets are much more efficiently achieved by increasing antenna aperture than by increasing HPA power alone.
    \item \textbf{Physical / Engineering Interpretation:} Transmit gain increases by $6$~dB for every doubling of the antenna diameter. This focusing gain multiplies the raw HPA wattage, allowing a major reduction in actual transmitter power to achieve the same equivalent radiated power (EIRP).
    \item \textbf{Design Guidelines:} HPA hardware costs scale steeply with output power. Ground terminal designers use this trade-off to minimize capital costs by choosing larger dish antennas, which also narrows the beamwidth and reduces sidelobe interference to adjacent satellites.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Combined $(E_b/N_0)$ vs. Information Bit Rate and Bandwidth}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{04_total_ebn0_bitrate_vs_bandwidth.png}
    \caption{Effective end-to-end $E_b/(N_0+I_0)$ vs. information bit rate and bandwidth.}
    \label{fig:contour04}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Setup / Objective:} Analyze how the combined end-to-end signal energy-per-bit ($E_b/N_0$) is affected by changes in the information data transmission rate ($2$~Mbps to $60$~Mbps) and the allocated noise bandwidth ($6$~MHz to $72$~MHz).
    \item \textbf{Numerical Observations:} The $E_b/N_0$ is determined primarily by bit rate. For the clear-sky combined $C/N_0$ of approximately $81.15$~dB-Hz, $E_b/N_0$ decreases from approximately $18.1$~dB at $2$~Mbps down to approximately $4.2$~dB at $50$~Mbps. At the nominal $10$~Mbps operating point, the clear-sky noise-only $E_b/N_0$ is approximately $11.15$~dB. Including adjacent-satellite interference and IMD reduces the effective value to approximately $10.69$~dB. The bandwidth axis does not independently change $E_b/N_0$ for a fixed $C/N_0$ and bit rate; rather, it constrains the feasible symbol rate, occupied bandwidth, and MODCOD selection. The operating-point marker confirms the effective result consistent with the ASI/IMD-included effective result summarized in Table~\ref{tab:comparison_summary}.
    \item \textbf{Physical / Engineering Interpretation:} Since $E_b/N_0 = C/N_0 - 10\log_{10}(R_b)$, sharing a finite carrier power $C$ over a higher number of bits per second ($R_b$) directly reduces the energy allocated to each individual bit. Bandwidth determines the maximum symbol rate and occupied spectrum, but does not alter the $E_b/N_0$ value at fixed $C/N_0$ and $R_b$.
    \item \textbf{Design Guidelines:} System engineers use this chart to select the maximum data rate that can be supported without dropping the $E_b/N_0$ below the required FEC threshold. For data rates beyond approximately $20$~Mbps, the link requires either additional satellite EIRP or a more efficient MODCOD to maintain positive margin.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Advanced Impairment and Dynamic Analysis}
This section introduces real-world atmospheric, orbital, and non-linear impairments to evaluate the system under worst-case operational conditions.
\subsubsection[Interference (ASI and IMD)]{Interference (ASI and IMD)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Setup / Objective:} Evaluate the combined link degradation when Adjacent-Satellite Interference (ASI) and Traveling-Wave Tube Amplifier (TWTA) Intermodulation Distortion (IMD) are enabled. Adjacent satellites are modeled at $11.0^\circ$E and $15.0^\circ$E longitudes relative to our target satellite at $13.0^\circ$E.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Observations:} Enabling ASI and IMD drives the combined Carrier-to-Noise-and-Interference ratio down, reducing the total operating margin from the clear-sky baseline of $\marginClear$~dB down to \textbf{\marginASI~dB}, a penalty of approximately $0.46$~dB.
    \item \textbf{Physical / Engineering Interpretation:} Co-channel power leakage from the adjacent satellites and non-linear intermodulation products generated within the satellite's multicarrier transponder act as extra noise sources, imposing a $0.46$~dB power penalty on the link for this specific geometry. The penalty is relatively modest here because of the $2^\circ$ GEO spacing and the well-sized 2.4~m uplink dish that provides strong off-axis rejection. In denser arcs or with smaller ground dishes, this penalty can be several dB.
    \item \textbf{Design Guidelines:} This result demonstrates that satellite operators cannot design links in isolation. Proper antenna sidelobe roll-off (to suppress ASI) and sufficient TWTA Output Back-Off (OBO $\approx 2.5$~dB to suppress IMD) must be strictly implemented to protect the link.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection[ITU-R Rain Fade and Atmospheric Impairments]{ITU-R Rain Fade and Atmospheric Impairments}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Setup / Objective:} Compute the excess atmospheric attenuation along the slant path under a $99.9\%$ link availability requirement (annual exceedance probability $p = 0.1\%$) using the ITU-R propagation standards.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Observations:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Uplink Attenuation (14.0 GHz):} Total combined atmospheric fade of \upRainAtten~dB.
        \item \textbf{Downlink Attenuation (12.0 GHz):} Total combined atmospheric fade of \downRainAtten~dB.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physical / Engineering Interpretation:} Rain is the dominant cause of attenuation in the Ku-band, contributing to over $90\%$ of the total fade depth. Scintillation and cloud losses represent secondary effects, while gaseous absorption is relatively static.
    \item \textbf{Design Guidelines:} At the $99.9\%$ availability design point, the combined link margin falls to a negative \textbf{\marginRain~dB} (with interference enabled). The baseline design does not achieve link closure at this fixed-threshold operating point. This indicates that Ku-band networks in Southern Europe and Anatolia require dynamic fade mitigation strategies such as DVB-S2 ACM, ULPC, or hardware upgrades (larger GS2 dish or higher satellite EIRP) to approach a strict 99.9\% fixed-rate availability target.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection[DVB-S2 ACM Throughput Performance]{DVB-S2 ACM Throughput Performance}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Setup / Objective:} Evaluate how the DVB-S2 Adaptive Coding and Modulation (ACM) selector changes the transmission format during the $99.9\%$ rain-fade case and attempts degraded-mode operation when the fixed-rate margin becomes negative.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Observations:} In clear-sky conditions, the link supports high-order spectral efficiencies. However, during the peak rain event, the ACM selector dynamically downshifts the MODCOD to highly robust \textbf{QPSK 1/2} (which requires a threshold $E_s/N_0$ of $1.00$~dB), attempting to maintain link closure with a reduced net throughput of \textbf{\throughputRain~Mbps}.
    \item \textbf{Physical / Engineering Interpretation:} By moving from QPSK 3/4 to the more robust QPSK 1/2 mode, the receiver operates at a lower required $E_s/N_0$ and accepts a reduced net throughput of \throughputRain~Mbps under the selected rain-fade case.
    \item \textbf{Design Guidelines:} This result shows why ACM is useful during fades: the link can trade throughput for a lower required $E_s/N_0$ and attempt degraded-mode operation when the fixed-rate margin is no longer positive, improving throughput during favorable link conditions while reducing outage risk during fades.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance Comparison and Engineering Synthesis}
To fully understand the robustness of the designed spacecraft communication link, it is critical to compare the static theoretical baseline against the advanced operational model. 

\begin{tcolorbox}[
    colback=gray!5,
    colframe=gray!45,
    title=Design Implication: Fixed-Rate Margin vs. ACM Throughput,
    fonttitle=\bfseries,
    boxrule=0.4pt,
    arc=2pt
]
\small
\textbf{Fixed-rate metric:} $E_b/N_0 = C/N_0 - 10\log_{10}(R_b)$ \\
\textbf{ACM metric:} $E_s/N_0 = C/N_0 - 10\log_{10}(R_s)$ \\
\textbf{Symbol rate:} $R_s = B / (1 + \alpha)$ \\
\textbf{Effective impaired metric:} Thermal noise and interference are combined using inverse-linear summation.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\textbf{Clarification:} The fixed-rate 10 Mbps case and the DVB-S2 ACM throughput case are not evaluated with the same closure criterion. The fixed-rate case uses a conservative required $E_b/N_0 = 7$ dB design threshold. The ACM case converts the available end-to-end $C/(N+I)_0$ into $E_s/N_0$ at the DVB-S2 symbol rate and checks closure against MODCOD-specific DVB-S2 thresholds plus implementation margin. Therefore, the ACM throughput values are estimated variable-rate DVB-S2 carrier capacities under the selected MODCOD, not a direct continuation of the fixed 10 Mbps operating mode.
\end{tcolorbox}

  \textbf{Interpretation of the Comparison:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Impact of Non-Linearities and Congestion:} Moving from a purely thermal-noise limited system to an interference-limited system (ASI + IMD) imposes an immediate $\approx 0.46$ dB power penalty. This demonstrates that simple static calculations may lead to optimistic margin estimates if adjacent orbital slots are congested or if the transponder is driven near saturation.
    \item \textbf{The Dominance of Atmospheric Fade:} The introduction of ITU-R rain fade models drastically alters the system state. The severe attenuation in the Ku-band consumes the available \marginASI~dB margin, reducing the margin to a \marginRain~dB margin deficit at 99.9\% availability. This indicates that the current hardware sizing is technically insufficient for the strict 99.9\% availability target, requiring either more satellite EIRP, a larger GS2 dish, or relaxing the availability target.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Compensation Strategy:} When the physical margin runs out during deep fade, the DVB-S2 ACM selector downshifts to the more robust QPSK 1/2 MODCOD, allowing operation at reduced throughput under lower $E_s/N_0$ conditions. This reduces the net throughput to \throughputRain~Mbps and attempts degraded-mode operation when the fixed-rate 10 Mbps margin is no longer positive.
\end{enumerate}

In synthesis, the staged analysis shows that the baseline link has positive clear-sky margin and can tolerate the selected moderate impairment cases. The static phase confirms that the link closes under clear-sky assumptions, while the advanced phase shows where the margin becomes insufficient under the 99.9\% rain-fade design point. ACM can then be interpreted as a degraded-mode mitigation, but not as proof of guaranteed fixed-rate service availability. However, strict 99.9\% fixed-rate link closure is not achieved by the baseline configuration (the fixed-rate margin falls to \marginRain~dB) and requires additional RF margin, larger receive aperture, or higher EIRP.
