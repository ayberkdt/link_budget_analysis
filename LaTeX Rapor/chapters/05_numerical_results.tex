\subsection{Analysis of 2D Contour Maps}
The Python analyzer generates five standard contour figures in `outputs/plots/` to study system sensitivity and trade-offs. They represent a core requirement of the term project:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{01\_downlink\_cn0\_dish\_vs\_frequency:} Plots Downlink $C/N_0$ on the Z-axis, with GS2 antenna diameter on the Y-axis ($0.5$m to $3.0$m) and downlink frequency on the X-axis ($10.7$ GHz to $12.75$ GHz).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Physical Meaning:} As the antenna diameter increases, the receiver gain increases, causing the $C/N_0$ to rise. As frequency increases, even though the FSPL rises, the receive antenna gain rises faster (proportional to $f^2$), yielding a net increase in $C/N_0$ towards the top-right corner.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{02\_downlink\_margin\_eirp\_vs\_tsys:} Plots Downlink Link Margin on the Z-axis, with satellite downlink EIRP on the X-axis ($40$ dBW to $55$ dBW) and GS2 alıcı gürültü sıcaklığı ($T_{sys}$) on the Y-axis ($50$ K to $300$ K). A bold red line denotes the $0$ dB closure limit.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Physical Meaning:} The link margin increases with higher satellite EIRP and lower receiver noise temperature. This plot is essential for design selection: any combination to the right of the $0$ dB contour line successfully closes the downlink, and the spacing helps size the margin cushion.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{03\_uplink\_cn\_power_vs\_dish:} Plots Uplink $C/N$ on the Z-axis over transponder bandwidth, with GS1 HPA output power on the X-axis ($5$ W to $100$ W) and GS1 antenna diameter on the Y-axis ($1.2$m to $4.5$m).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Physical Meaning:} Sizing the uplink transmitter requires a trade-off: a larger ground antenna allows a lower power amplifier (HPA) to achieve the same target uplink $C/N$, protecting adjacent satellites from excessive boresight leakage.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{04\_total\_ebn0\_bitrate\_vs\_bandwidth:} Plots Combined $E_b/N_0$ on the Z-axis, with allocated transponder bandwidth on the Y-axis ($10$ MHz to $50$ MHz) and information bit rate on the X-axis ($2$ Mbps to $50$ Mbps).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Physical Meaning:} Displays the classic power-vs-bandwidth trade-off. As bit rate increases, the energy-per-bit drops ($E_b/N_0 = C/N_0 - 10\log R_b$), indicating that high data rates require more carrier power.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{05\_gs2\_elevation\_latitude\_vs\_longitude:} Plots the elevation angle from GS2 to the GEO satellite as a function of ground coordinates (X-axis: longitude difference $-60^\circ$ to $+60^\circ$; Y-axis: latitude $0^\circ$ to $70^\circ$N).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Physical Meaning:} Defines the geographic coverage visibility window. Higher latitudes or larger longitude separations push the ground station towards the edge of visibility, where the elevation drops below the standard $5^\circ$ or $10^\circ$ engineering limit.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Advanced Analysis Results [EXTRA]}

\subsubsection{Interference (ASI and IMD) [EXTRA]}
When Adjacent-Satellite Interference (ASI) and transponder intermodulation (IMD) are enabled, the combined carrier-to-interference ratio $C/I$ is evaluated. For komşu uydu longitudes of $40^\circ$E and $44^\circ$E (nominal satellite at $42^\circ$E), the combined interference-included margin falls to \textbf{3.43 dB}. This demonstrates that adjacent-system leakage imposes a $0.50$ dB penalty on our link margin, highlighting the necessity of proper side-lobe suppression.

\subsubsection{Orbital Motion Sensitivity [EXTRA]}
The apparent GEO station-keeping motion simulation shows that over a 48-hour window, the satellite distance fluctuates periodically by up to $77.9$ km, causing the downlink FSPL to vary by $0.018$ dB. This small variation indicates that geostationary orbit keeping is highly deterministic, and standard engineering margins (e.g. $0.5$ dB pointing allowance) easily cover these variations.

\subsubsection{ITU-R Availability and Outage Curves [EXTRA]}
Under the standards-based ITU-R propagation availability study, total slant-path attenuation at $99.9\%$ availability (exceedance $p=0.1\%$) is computed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Uplink Attenuation ($14.0$ GHz):} $3.83$ dB (comprising $3.54$ dB rain, $0.11$ dB gas, $0.16$ dB cloud, and $0.27$ dB scintillation).
    \item \textbf{Downlink Attenuation ($12.0$ GHz):} $2.65$ dB (comprising $2.43$ dB rain, $0.09$ dB gas, $0.11$ dB cloud, and $0.26$ dB scintillation).
\end{itemize}
Under these faded conditions, the combined margin falls to \textbf{0.32 dB}. This closes the link, but with a highly restricted margin, proving that Ku-band links in temperate climates require careful fade mitigation.

\subsubsection{DVB-S2 ACM Throughput Trade-offs [EXTRA]}
To prevent outage under these ITU fades, the DVB-S2 ACM selector dynamically adjusts the carrier format. Under open-sky conditions, the link supports high spectral efficiency MODCODs. However, during the $99.9\%$ availability rain event, the selector downshifts the downlink carrier to \textbf{QPSK 1/2} (theoretical required Es/N0 of $1.00$ dB) to maintain the link with a net throughput of \textbf{29.66 Mbps}, demonstrating a highly robust adaptive mitigation strategy.
