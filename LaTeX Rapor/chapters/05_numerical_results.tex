The Python analyzer generates the standard contour figures to study system sensitivity and trade-offs. These plots represent a core requirement of the term project:

    \caption{Downlink C/N0 vs GS2 Dish Diameter and Frequency.}
    \label{fig:contour01}
\end{figure}
As shown in Şekil~\ref{fig:contour01}, the Z-axis represents Downlink $C/N_0$ in dB-Hz, with GS2 antenna diameter on the Y-axis ($0.5$m to $3.0$m) and downlink frequency on the X-axis ($10.7$ GHz to $12.75$ GHz).
As the antenna diameter increases, the receiver gain increases, causing the $C/N_0$ to rise. As frequency increases, even though the FSPL rises, the receive antenna gain rises faster (proportional to $f^2$), yielding a net increase in $C/N_0$ towards the top-right corner of the contour map.

The link margin increases with higher satellite EIRP and lower receiver noise temperature. This plot is essential for design selection: any combination to the right of the $0$ dB contour line successfully closes the downlink, and the spacing helps size the margin cushion.

\subsection[Advanced Analysis Results]{Advanced Analysis Results\textsuperscript{*}}

\subsubsection[Interference (ASI and IMD)]{Interference (ASI and IMD)\textsuperscript{*}}
When Adjacent-Satellite Interference (ASI) and transponder intermodulation (IMD) are enabled, the combined carrier-to-interference ratio $C/I$ is evaluated. For komşu uydu longitudes of $11^\circ$E and $15^\circ$E (nominal satellite at $13^\circ$E), the combined interference-included margin falls to \textbf{4.16 dB}. This demonstrates that adjacent-system leakage imposes a $0.51$ dB penalty on our link margin, highlighting the necessity of proper side-lobe suppression.

\subsubsection[Orbital Motion Sensitivity]{Orbital Motion Sensitivity\textsuperscript{*}}
The apparent GEO station-keeping motion simulation shows that over a 48-hour window, the satellite distance fluctuates periodically by up to $83.6$ km for downlink, causing the downlink FSPL to vary by $0.019$ dB. This small variation indicates that geostationary orbit keeping is highly deterministic, and standard engineering margins (e.g. $0.5$ dB pointing allowance) easily cover these variations.

\subsubsection[ITU-R Availability and Outage Curves]{ITU-R Availability and Outage Curves\textsuperscript{*}}
Under the standards-based ITU-R propagation availability study, total slant-path attenuation at $99.9\%$ availability (exceedance $p=0.1\%$) is computed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Uplink Attenuation ($14.0$ GHz):} $3.76$ dB (comprising $3.47$ dB rain, $0.11$ dB gas, $0.16$ dB cloud, and $0.27$ dB scintillation).
    \item \textbf{Downlink Attenuation ($12.0$ GHz):} $2.76$ dB (comprising $2.52$ dB rain, $0.10$ dB gas, $0.13$ dB cloud, and $0.28$ dB scintillation).
\end{itemize}
Under these faded conditions, the combined margin falls to \textbf{0.30 dB} (with interference). This closes the link, but with a highly restricted margin, proving that Ku-band links in temperate climates require careful fade mitigation.

\subsubsection[DVB-S2 ACM Throughput Trade-offs]{DVB-S2 ACM Throughput Trade-offs\textsuperscript{*}}
To prevent outage under these ITU fades, the DVB-S2 ACM selector dynamically adjusts the carrier format. Under open-sky conditions, the link supports high spectral efficiency MODCODs. However, during the $99.9\%$ availability rain event, the selector downshifts the downlink carrier to \textbf{QPSK 1/2} (theoretical required Es/N0 of $1.00$ dB) to maintain the link with a net throughput of \textbf{29.67 Mbps}, demonstrating a highly robust adaptive mitigation strategy.
